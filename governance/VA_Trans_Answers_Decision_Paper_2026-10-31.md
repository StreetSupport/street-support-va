# How the VA treats trans people when it filters services

*A short decision paper, 31 October 2026. For the technical lead first, and for the board if needed. The detail, the law and the sources are in the working paper "How the VA handles trans answers when filtering services" (revision 4, 31 October 2026), which goes with this one.*

## The decision in one line

When someone tells the Virtual Assistant they are a trans woman or a trans man, should that ever stop the VA showing them a women-only or men-only service?

## Why this has come up

The VA asks about gender because some services are for women only or men only. It uses the answer to avoid showing people services that say they cannot use them.

The way it did this was broken. It looked for the letters "male" inside the answer, and "female" contains "male". So women were hidden from women's services. Trans women and trans men were hidden from them too. Nobody decided that: it was a fault.

That fault is now fixed, and the fix is waiting for the technical lead's review. But the fix still has to do something with the answers "Trans Female" and "Trans Male". For now it hides nothing from either. That is a placeholder, not a decision. This paper is about the decision.

## Three things that are fixed

These are the Managing Director's starting points (31 October 2026).

1. **We have to ask about gender.** Some services are for one sex only. Without the question, the VA cannot respect that.
2. **We cannot ask only "male or female".** Some people cannot answer that honestly. They would give a wrong answer or stop using the VA, and the VA would fail the people it is there for.
3. **We will not show whether an organisation accepts trans people.** That would push organisations into taking a public position on a contested issue. It is not SSN's place, and it is not what the platform is for.

## What the law says, briefly

*This is a plain summary of published legal commentary, not legal advice.*

- In April 2025 the Supreme Court decided that "sex" in the Equality Act means biological sex.
- Updated guidance for service providers came into force on 5 August 2026. It says a single-sex service is based on biological sex, and that each provider decides, case by case, whether single-sex provision is right for its service.
- So the decision about who can use a single-sex service belongs to the service, not to anyone who lists it. Nothing found places a duty on a directory or signposting service like SSN to filter people by sex.
- Parts of how the guidance applies are still being argued in the courts.

## What SSN's own rules already say

The VA's Service Matching Contract lists the only reasons the VA may hide a service. One is a clear clash: the service says it is for women only and the person has said they are male, or the other way round. Answers such as "prefer not to say" and "non-binary" must never hide anything.

A person who answers "Trans Female" or "Trans Male" has not said "male" or "female". So, on the contract as written, nothing is hidden from them. Hiding services from them would add a new reason to the list. The contract says that needs a review, and that material changes to matching need the trustees.

SSN's neutrality principle points the same way: we show what exists and do not judge the organisations that provide it.

## The options

| | What the VA does | What a trans woman sees | What a trans man sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Hides nothing for a trans answer | Every service | Every service |
| **B** | Treats the answer by sex at birth | Not women-only services | Not men-only services |
| **C** | Treats the answer by the gender the person gives | Not men-only services | Not women-only services |

**Option A: hide nothing.**
- *Good:* nobody is wrongly hidden from help. The VA makes no judgement about who a service should admit; the service does. It is what the contract already says. It stays right whichever way the law develops.
- *Risk:* a trans woman may be shown a women-only service that turns her away. That costs her a contact, and it may be a hard moment.

**Option B: by sex at birth.**
- *Good:* it follows the default in the new guidance, so fewer people are shown services that would turn them away.
- *Risk:* the VA makes each service's decision for it, although the guidance says that decision is the service's. It shows a trans man women-only services as if they were open to him, when the guidance says they may not be. It adds a new reason to hide services, so it needs the trustees.

**Option C: by the gender the person gives.**
- *Good:* it reflects how the person describes themselves.
- *Risk:* it shows a trans woman women-only services as open to her, against the default in the guidance, and hides men-only services from her. Like B, it makes the services' decision for them, and needs the trustees.

**A possible addition to A: repeat what the service already says.** Where a service is labelled "women only" or "men only", the VA could show that alongside it: "This service says it is for women only. Contact them to check before you go." This says nothing about trans people. It repeats only what the service already says publicly, so it stays within point 3 above. It helps a person decide before making a contact. It only works where services have labelled themselves; some have not.

## A separate fault in the question itself

The longer housing route asks about "current biological gender", then offers the answers Male, Female, Trans Female and Trans Male. That mixes sex and gender in one question. It also has no "prefer not to say", unlike the other gender questions in the VA. And it asks about pregnancy after "Female" and "Trans Female" but not after "Trans Male". These need fixing whatever is decided here, within points 1 and 2 above. That belongs to the review of the VA's questions.

## Recommendation

*Proposed, not decided.*

**Option A, with the possible addition, and the question wording fixed.**

Why:
- It is what SSN's existing rules already say.
- It leaves each service's decision with the service, and keeps SSN neutral.
- If the VA gets it wrong, the cost is a wasted contact, not a person hidden from help.
- It needs no change if the law moves again.

## Who decides

Under SSN's Governance Decision Framework:
- **Option A** applies the contract as it stands. That is likely a matter for the technical lead, with the trustees told.
- **Options B and C** add a new reason to hide services. That changes a governing principle and goes to the board.
- **The addition and the question wording** are new things a person sees. The technical lead's review should say whether they need more than his agreement.

## What we are asking

**Of the technical lead:**
1. Does he agree that option A is what the contract already says?
2. Does option A need anything more than his agreement and the trustees being told?
3. Is the possible addition worth building?

**Of the board, if it goes there:**
1. Which option, A, B or C?
2. Any view on the possible addition.
