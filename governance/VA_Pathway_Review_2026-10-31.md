### 2.3 How age works, and a proposal

**How it works now.** The VA turns each band into its lowest age: 16, 18, 21 or 25. An entry is shown when that number sits inside its `ageMin` to `ageMax`. So the bands, not the services, set the precision. A service running to 25 is set to 24, because the VA cannot tell a 25-year-old from anyone older.

**Two small inconsistencies to tidy when the code is next touched:**
- The over-25 band is stored as "25 or over" in one place and "25+" in another. The older people filter only recognises "25+". Item 80 removes the older people group, so this goes with that work.
- The full route asks age twice in principle (an early broad band and the detailed band in Section C), but the early question is no longer reached, so the full route has no age until Section C.

**Proposed.** Ask age once, early, on both routes, with the bands set by the endpoints that differ: Under 16, 16-17, 18-24, 25 or over. Split 18-24 into 18-20 and 21-24 only when it changes an endpoint, which today means only for care leavers (section 5). This follows the pathway principle in section 4: the question asked is the one that separates the endpoints still possible.

## 3. The quick route

### 3.1 Its two purposes

1. **Data minimisation.** Every question needs a documented purpose. For most questions that purpose is routing, and a routing question that does not change someone's route has no basis. Safeguarding, accessibility and other agreed operational purposes are recorded separately. The Quick Route Design Note (13 March 2026) and the Data Minimisation Note record this.
2. **Completion.** A person with no complicating circumstances should not sit through a long run of questions answering "no" or "not applicable". It is slower than searching the website, and people give up. The VA has to serve the simple case as well as it serves multiple disadvantage, or it does not serve everyone. *(Managing Director, 31 October 2026. Not yet in any document.)*

The two point the same way. Both say: only ask what changes where the person is sent.

### 3.2 What was designed

- **WatsonX** showed a list of circumstances before detailed questions. If none applied, the person took the quick route; if any did, the guided route. WatsonX's code is not in either repo: every file on every branch of both was listed, and the VA repo's text was searched for "watson". It survives as the design note's description.
- **The new VA carries the assumption.** The route choice screen (`GATE2_ROUTE_SELECTION`) says the full route asks about health, immigration, convictions and other circumstances. It says the quicker route will assume the answers are "no" or "none". This has been in the code since 1 February 2026.
- **The design note's six circumstances:** significant health or mental health concerns; immigration status not straightforward; pregnant or have children; LGBTQ+; previous convictions; aged 16 to 20 and previously looked after.
- **The note's other parts:** a switch from quick to guided at any time, re-evaluating forward from the point of change; and null answers on the quick route meaning "confirmed does not apply".

### 3.3 Where the build is

- The choice only changes anything for Emergency Housing. Other needs ask the same questions on both routes.
- The screen names three circumstances, not the six. Backlog item 62 is to list them.
- The switch is not built yet.
- On the quick route a homeless person answers four questions after choosing housing. A person at home but at risk answers ten or eleven, because the prevention questions sit on both routes.

### 3.4 Proposal: one circumstances screen instead of two routes

**Proposed.** Replace the binary choice with the screen the design always intended, and let what is ticked open only the questions for that pathway:

> "A few things change where we can point you. Do any of these apply? Tick any, or choose None of these."
> - I'm pregnant or have children with me
> - I've been in care (aged 16 to 24)
> - I'm LGBTQ+ and would like services that know that
> - My immigration status is not straightforward
> - I have a health or mental health need that affects where I can live
> - I have a criminal conviction that affects housing
> - None of these
> - I'd rather not say

**How it routes:**
- **None of these.** The quick route as now: the core questions only, with the assumption stated on screen as it is today.
- **One or more ticked.** Only that pathway's questions are asked, not the whole full route. A care leaver answers the care questions; they are not asked about ethnicity or physical health.
- **I'd rather not say.** The core questions, and results that are not narrowed by anything assumed. A declined answer widens, never narrows.
- **A way back.** At the results, one line: "If something on that list does apply, tell me and I'll check again." This is the switch the design note describes, re-evaluating forward.

**Why it meets both purposes.** The majority, with nothing ticked, get the shortest route. Everyone else is asked only what changes their result. No one answers "no" to a list of questions one at a time.

**What stays fixed.** The crisis gate first; area; who it is for; age with the under-16 option. These come before the screen whatever is ticked.

**Governance.** The screen asks the person to self-identify into categories, some of them special category data (health, sexual orientation). It replaces individual questions rather than adding to them. Whether ticking an item meets the explicit consent the Data Minimisation Note relies on is not settled: choosing an answer and giving valid explicit consent are not necessarily the same. It needs a data protection check against the Appropriate Policy Document (D11), and James's review of the routing.

**Status.** Worth developing, not yet a replacement. The current quick route stays until the screen has been tested with users.

**What testing judges.** The quick route's purpose is completion, not only brevity. So the screen is judged on whether people:
- understand the choices and why they are asked;
- can decline without losing relevant options;
- reach a useful next step, and do not give up on the way.

Fewer questions asked is a means, not the measure.

**Failure modes.**
- People skim a list. Put the screen after age and area, keep it to the items that genuinely change an endpoint, and keep "None of these" last.
- A person may not know they count, for example as a care leaver. Each item can carry a one-line explanation, and the care questions offer "Not sure".
- The screen may itself feel like a request to disclose. Someone who only wants basic housing help sees sexuality, immigration and convictions named straight away. User testing should check how it feels, and whether an explanation of why we ask comes first.
- "None of these" may not mean none apply: a person may not recognise themselves in the wording. The way back at the results line covers this in part. "Not sure" and "Prefer not to say" are handled the same way across the screen and the pathways.
- Domestic abuse is deliberately not on the list. It is caught by the crisis gate and the reason question, and putting it on a tick list makes disclosure casual. Proposed: keep it off, and add free-text detection (section 6).

## 4. The VA as pathways: the routing principle

### 4.1 The principle

*(Managing Director, 31 October 2026, as refined after review.)* Each housing outcome has a complete pathway ending in a useful set of next steps, not a single destination. A care leaver, for example, may see a leaving care team, Housing Options, a youth navigator and Shelter together. The routing questions work out which pathway a person is on. At each point the VA asks only what still changes which next steps are shown, and recommends once a single set remains.

Every question needs a documented purpose: routing, safeguarding, accessibility or another agreed operational reason. Routing necessity is the necessity test for data protection; the lawful basis and special category conditions are established separately.

### 4.2 What the code shows today

Every question the VA can ask was traced to what it changes downstream.

- **Questions that change nothing shown: 18.**
  - Early flow: preferred name, access location, returning user and its follow-up.
  - Housing: Housing Options involvement (marked to be wired in), employment and prior support in prevention, duration, income, prior use, already supported, and which organisation.
  - Section C: ethnicity, physical health, convictions, currently in care.
  - Other: LGBTQ+ specialist preference on non-housing, and the children question on the DA path.
- **Three of those** (access location and the two returning-user questions) have their own operational-intelligence basis, so they need review rather than removal.
- **Four collect special category data with no routing effect:** ethnicity, physical health, pregnancy on the DA path, and mental health on the housing route.
- **Several are asked when the remaining endpoints are already settled.** Examples: the children question in prevention when the situation is not urgent, and four of the five sleeping situations, which give identical results.

### 4.3 Proposal: endpoint elimination

**Proposed, design only.** It changes architecture, so it goes to James. Section 4.4 sets out how it would be trialled without becoming a launch dependency.

**Three layers.**
1. **A fixed safeguarding layer that elimination never touches.** The crisis gate first; under 16 as option 2; mid-conversation age detection before any classifier; the under-16 option on every age question; the exits exactly as written. Protective prompts, such as the prevention "anything else going on" question, are flagged so they are always asked.
2. **A pathway table held as governed data.** For each pathway: what triggers it (conditions on answers), its endpoints starting from the floor (Housing Options and Shelter), extra endpoints that depend on particular answers, and its governing contract section.
3. **A function.** Given the answers so far, it works out which endpoint sets are still possible. It asks the next question that actually narrows them, choosing the less sensitive question where two would do the same job. It recommends as soon as one set of next steps is left.

**Rules.**
- The pathway table governs how the VA navigates. The live platform governs which services and access routes exist. The table never holds organisations, contact details or eligibility assumptions.
- A question no pathway refers to is never asked.
- A declined answer keeps all candidates.
- A data gap (no local service) is an explicit endpoint, so the VA still asks the question and records the gap.
- Exit wording never changes; the engine decides which exit is reached, not what it says.

**How it fits section 3.** The circumstances screen is the first narrowing question for most people. It is the least sensitive way to rule several pathways out at once.

**Failure modes.**
- Losing protective questions: the protective flag, changeable only through the Safeguarding Contract.
- A pathway silently unreachable: build-time checks for reachability, the housing floor and missing phrases, reviewed against hand-written user scenarios.
- Fewer questions means fewer moments to disclose: free-text detection on every message.
- Explainability: a published question tree for each release, and a per-session trace in category codes only.

### 4.4 The referee in plain English, and how to trial it

**What it is.** Today the VA follows a script: a flowchart of questions written into the code, box by box. It often carries on asking after it already knows enough, because that is the next box. The alternative is a list and a referee:
- **The list.** For each situation, the next steps that person should see, and what would tell us they are in that situation.
- **The referee.** A small piece of code. After each answer it asks: which situations are still possible, and is there a question that would tell them apart? If so, it asks the gentlest one. If not, it shows the next steps.

It works like twenty questions: only ask what narrows things down, and stop when you know. The safety layer (section 4.3, layer 1) stays outside it.

**How big it is.** The referee itself is small. The work is moving what the housing section already does into the list, which is mostly copying existing decisions rather than inventing new ones.

**Why trial it rather than defer it.** Building care leavers, the domestic abuse housing layer and places to start into the script, then moving them to the list later, wires them twice. The wording, endpoints and tests carry over either way, so it is not double the work, but it is avoidable rework.

**What makes a trial safe.**
- The tests describe what a person sees, not how the code works. The existing 137 tests and the 77 age scenarios run on 31 October would all still apply.
- The referee only takes over after someone chooses housing. The crisis gate, location, who it is for and the under-16 checks stay exactly as they are.
- It is built on a separate branch, so nothing live changes.

**Three stages.** Agreed after the second review, 31 October 2026.
1. **Exploration.** Claude works out what the referee can do, how much work it is, and whether it improves the existing pathways. It needs little of the Managing Director's time.
2. **Evaluation.** The Managing Director and Claude review the findings together, mainly whether journeys are better and unnecessary questions fewer.
3. **Adoption.** The Managing Director and James decide whether it is ready for December, or stays a future improvement. The current VA remains the fallback, so the referee cannot become an unplanned dependency.

**What the exploration delivers.** A short, decision-ready comparison, not an architecture report:
- the existing and proposed routing run on the same user scenarios;
- whether it asks fewer questions without losing useful next steps;
- what it changes near the safeguarding boundaries;
- a realistic estimate of the extra integration and testing work before launch;
- a recommendation, with reasons.

**The exploration (started 31 October 2026).** It runs in the background with little of the Managing Director's time. It is reviewed together once there is something worth looking at, not on a fixed schedule.
1. Build the referee for the housing section only, on a branch.
2. Run the existing tests and the age scenarios against it, and count the questions asked.
3. **If they pass and fewer questions are asked:** the results go to you and James as evidence for a separate decision. A successful trial does not on its own authorise using the referee for launch. That decision weighs the integration risk ahead of the first end-to-end slice. It also weighs whether the referee handles what the existing tests do not cover: declined answers, overlapping circumstances and the new pathways. Scenarios for those are written before the decision.
4. **If it gets messy, or tests fail in ways that are hard to fix:** drop it and apply the principle by hand, having lost little.
5. Decide before the first end-to-end slice in the week of 16 November, so the WMCA trial does not carry an untested change. If the answer is not yet, the referee stays on its branch and the launch pathways are built on the current VA.

**Failure modes of the trial.** It can absorb time meant for launch work, so it is bounded by the decision date. Passing tests prove only what the tests cover, so James reviews the referee itself, not only the results.

## 5. Care leavers

### 5.1 The law at 31 October 2026 (England)

| Who | What is owed | By whom | Where the person goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16-17, never in care | Children's services' duty to accommodate (s.20, Southwark) takes precedence; housing must assess, not simply refer on | Children's services where the young person is now | Children's services or Housing Options where they are |
| 16-17, in care now | Looked-after duties, pathway plan | The council looking after them, which may be elsewhere | Social worker, or that council's children's services |
| 16-17, left care (relevant child) | Accommodation and maintenance, personal adviser. From 30 September 2026 cannot be found intentionally homeless | The council that last looked after them | Personal adviser or leaving care team, and Housing Options where they are |
| 18-20, care leaver | Personal adviser and pathway plan to 21; help as welfare requires; Staying Put; priority need for homelessness, with exceptions; cannot be found intentionally homeless | Leaving care: the council that looked after them. Homelessness: the council where they apply | Personal adviser or leaving care team, and Housing Options |
| 21-24, care leaver | Personal adviser and support on request until 25; education support; priority need only if vulnerable; cannot be found intentionally homeless | As above | Leaving care team, and Housing Options |
| 25 and over | Leaving care duties end, except education support already running | Housing authority | Housing Options |

**What changed.** The Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 (Royal Assent 29 April 2026):
- Section 9, in force 30 September 2026: relevant children and care leavers under 25 cannot be found intentionally homeless.
- Section 8, in force 30 September 2026: the local offer must cover help to find and keep a home, financial support and early action to prevent homelessness.
- Section 7, Staying Close: enacted but not in force; guidance expects it in 2028 to 2029.

The Homelessness Code of Guidance changed on 30 September 2026, and Chapter 22 was rebuilt.

**Two things that shape the VA.**
- Leaving care sits with the council that looked after the person. Housing sits with the council where they are now. In the West Midlands these will often differ.
- The Act and the Code describe the transition differently: the Act turns on when the application was made, the Code on when the cause happened. That is one more reason the VA states that leaving care teams help with housing, and never what the person is owed.

**Not confirmed from primary text:** the commencement regulations themselves (dates come from the Act's annotations); Code paragraphs 14.32 and 15.40; the statute text of section 23CA.

## 6. Domestic abuse

### 6.1 How it works now

There are three ways in:
1. The crisis gate, option 4.
2. The reason for homelessness, full route only.
3. The prevention follow-up for people at home and at risk.

All three ask gender, then children, then end the session with a safeguarding exit:
- **The national line by gender:** National Domestic Abuse Helpline for women, ManKind for men, Galop for non-binary and others.
- **Always shown:** Shelter's domestic abuse page and the 999 line.
- **Local services by council:** shown for the second and third ways in only.

Local services come from `safeguarding-endpoints.json`; the national numbers are in the phrasebank by design.

### 6.2 Where it is not consistent

1. **Crisis-gate disclosures get no local services.** The area has not been asked yet. Under-16 and fire/flood ask for the area before their exit; domestic abuse does not.
2. **Crisis-gate disclosures are always worded to the person themselves**, even when a supporter is typing. Who it is for is asked later.
3. **The housing layer never appears.** The Housing Pathway Endpoints Contract describes a DA housing pathway: Housing Options, the Shelter helpline, a local DA service, and Children's Services where there are children. It says this runs alongside or after the safeguarding signposting. Because the exit ends the session, a person who asked for housing and disclosed abuse never sees Housing Options. The terminal's own DA block can never be reached.
4. **"Prefer not to say" on gender goes to Galop.** That treats a declined answer as a fact. A woman who declines loses the national women's line.
5. **Local services ignore gender.** Men in Birmingham and Solihull are shown Women's Aid, which the data marks as for women and children. Gender-aware notes in the data are not used.
6. **The children question changes nothing.** The contacts are the same either way, and it collects pregnancy, which is health data.
7. **The quick route has no DA question after the crisis gate** for people who are homeless.
8. **Two local datasets disagree.** `dv_local` and `dvOrgs` differ for Coventry, the Black Country and Birmingham.
9. **Galop's number.** The contract gives ManKind's number for Galop; the code uses a different one. It needs checking at Galop's own site.
10. **There is no domestic abuse category among the support needs,** so the 30 local DA services in the catalogue never surface. The Safeguarding Contract's free-text disclosure rule is not built yet for DA.
11. **Typed answers are matched loosely.** At the gender question, typing "male" selects Female, because "female" contains "male". Buttons are unaffected. A typed crisis-gate message containing "film" or "football" gets the out-of-scope reply.

### 6.3 Is local DA worth showing nationally?

**Yes, in nearly every area.** The national Safety pass ruled 62 domestic abuse listings as Safety (3 provisional), outside the WMCA. Counted by the areas each organisation covers:
- 21 of the 22 live areas outside the WMCA have at least one, usually 2 to 7. Manchester and Trafford have 7; Bury, Rochdale and Salford 6.
- Leeds, Liverpool, Luton and Southampton have 1 each. Wakefield has none.
- All seven WMCA councils already have a local entry in the VA.

Two caveats:
- The count is a floor. It covers only groups with an old domestic abuse tick, not the new Safety listings the pass found from text alone.
- It counts an organisation in every area it lists, so wide-coverage organisations may be counted where their DA service does not run.

Source: `old_safety_ledger.md` and the batch inputs on `feat/national-safety-batch9-2026-10-30`, against the live locations of 27 October.

### 6.4 Proposed DA pathway

**Order.**
1. The safety exit first, exactly as reviewed: the national line by gender, the Shelter DA page and 999. Help comes before any further question.
2. **Then location,** using the same step as everyone else: share location or enter a postcode. A list of councils does not scale nationally. *(Managing Director, 31 October 2026.)* The design does not depend on who may be present. Help is given first, location is optional (share, enter a postcode, or decline) and the exit button (section 6.5) covers the case where someone is present. These are proposed features for the safeguarding review, not a substitute for it.
3. **Then local services and housing:** local DA services in that area, Housing Options, the Shelter helpline, and Children's Services where there are children. This is the housing layer the contract already describes, following the exit rather than replaced by it.

**Where local services come from.** The live platform's Safety listings (Domestic abuse), as ruled in the Safety pass, filtered by who each serves. There is no separate per-council list to maintain, which also retires the two WMCA lists that disagree. Where an area has none, the national line covers it and the gap is recorded.

**Test scenario to add.** Someone who declines location still reaches a useful next step: the national line, Shelter, 999 and a way to find their council's Housing Options.

**Status.** The inconsistencies in 6.2 stand as findings. The replacement sequence stays provisional pending the safeguarding and technical review.

**Questions.**
- **Gender:** only if not already given; buttons only. Declined or unrecognised shows all three national lines.
- **Children:** kept, because with Children's Services as an endpoint it now changes the result. Pregnancy is dropped.

### 6.5 An exit button

**Proposed.** Add a quick exit, as many domestic abuse sites have. The GOV.UK Design System's "Exit this page" component is the standard pattern:
- one tap replaces the page with a neutral one;
- keyboard users press Shift three times.

It does not clear browser history. The guidance is to pair it with a short line on private browsing and clearing history.

**Options.**
- **A.** In the VA, shown from the moment someone chooses domestic abuse or sexual violence.
- **B.** Also on the website's Safety pages.
- **C.** On the VA at all times.

It touches the Safeguarding Contract and the Terms and Conditions, so it goes to James.

### 6.6 Statements that apply the law to the person

None of the DA exits do this. Elsewhere, graded:

**A: applies an entitlement or duty to this person.**
- Fire and flood: "This is a priority need - they must help you today", and "The council has a legal duty to help you find emergency accommodation".
- The 16-20 guidance: "you may be entitled to support from social services. They have a duty to help..."

**B: says what a body must do for this person.**
- The housing first step: "They have a legal duty to assess your situation".
- No recourse to public funds with family: Children's Services "have a duty to assess".
- Escalation with children: "Local councils have a duty... They should assess your situation urgently".
- Advice, sleeping tonight: "they have a duty to help if you're homeless tonight".

**C: general law written as "you".** Several advice entries, including the priority need explainer.

A should go. B can be rewritten as what the body does ("Housing Options assess..."). C is your call. The Safeguarding Contract's fire/flood row and its test header also use "priority need" and would align.

## 7. Supporter and professional variants

### 7.1 What it is

The question "Are you looking for support for yourself, or for someone else?" offers four answers:
- Myself;
- Someone I'm supporting;
- I'm a professional or organisation;
- Not sure.

They are stored as SELF, SUPPORTER, PROFESSIONAL and SELF.

**The answer changes wording only.** The services shown and the questions asked are the same for all three. Supporter and professional get "they" instead of "you". The professional wording differs only on the safeguarding exits:
- **under-16:** no NSPCC adult line and no warm sign-off;
- **domestic and sexual abuse:** no consolation, and "you'll know your next steps";
- **everywhere:** 999 on every exit.

Everywhere else professionals see the supporter wording.

### 7.2 Why it exists

It came out of the under-16 work in April and May 2026.
- A friend or relative is in a different position from a social worker or housing worker.
- Professionals have their own safeguarding duties and procedures, and the NSPCC adult line is meant for non-professionals.

James signed off the Supporter and Professional Language Review v1.1 on 8 May 2026. It is in the project files as a Word document, not in the repo.

### 7.3 Two things to know

- The crisis gate comes before the who-for question. Crisis exits chosen at the opening question are always in the first person, whoever is typing.
- The professional self-harm wording is written but not switched on (item 72, with James).

### 7.4 A worker sitting beside someone

None of the three variants was designed for this:
- **Self** assumes the reader is the person.
- **Supporter** talks about the person as if they are not there, while they may be reading it.
- **Professional** assumes a trained worker with a safeguarding lead. It drops the NSPCC line and the warm sign-off, and in one exit Childline.

**Options:**
- **A. Advise "Myself", with the person leading.** No code change. The worker's own answers are recorded as the person's.
- **B. Advise "Someone I'm supporting".** No code change. "They" is read by the person beside them.
- **C. A fourth answer, "I'm helping someone who is here with me".** It uses "you" to the person, with one line to the helper where action may fall to them, and keeps every helpline. It needs language review, phrasebank entries, tests and James. It fits within items 60 and 69.
- **D. Professional only where the worker has a safeguarding policy and lead.** No code change, but relies on workers judging their own position.

**Proposed: C.** It matches digital access as you describe it and keeps the person addressed directly.

## 8. Places to start as a prevention endpoint

### 8.1 What is settled

- **What a place to start is.** Somewhere a person can walk into or phone, without a referral, and be welcomed, oriented and intentionally connected onwards. They do not need to know what their problem is.
- **Who it is not for.** It is not for crisis.
- **Confirmation is consent.** An organisation is shown as a place to start only once it agrees.
- **The 29 October decision.** One pressure leads to the specialist for it. Several, or not knowing, lead to a place to start.
- **The working view.** A place to start is the prevention pathway's version of choosing to talk to a person rather than a machine (31 October 2026). Settled list for WMCA at go-live assumed.

### 8.2 What the VA does today

It has no place-to-start concept yet. Prevention is reached through Emergency Housing, then "not homeless", then "at home", then "at risk". It ends at the ordinary housing results.

### 8.3 Proposed: places to start in the prevention pathway

**Where it applies.** Only in prevention, and only early, while the VA is still working out where the person is on their journey.

**It becomes the endpoint when:**
- the person names several pressures;
- they are not sure what the problem is;
- they choose to talk to someone.

**It is never the endpoint for:**
- crisis or any safeguarding exit;
- under 16;
- a single clear pressure with a specialist.

For 16 and 17 year olds, children's services come first and a place to start may follow.

**How it is offered.**
- **First, as a choice.** Offered once at the start of prevention, after the crisis gate: "Would you rather talk this through with someone, in person or on the phone?" A person can choose it without explaining their circumstances first. Their preference is reason enough, even where the VA could have identified a specialist.
- **Then, as a routing outcome,** for several pressures or not sure.

Three questions are kept apart: availability (is there a confirmed place to start the platform can show?), presentation (when is it shown alongside specialist and statutory routes?) and choice (can someone ask for a person without explaining first?). The answer to the third is yes.

"In person or on the phone" matches the definition, which includes phoning.

**Questions.** At most two, beyond the crisis gate, area and who it is for:
1. **Position.** Home at risk now, worried it might be, or something else.
2. **Pressures.** Multi-select: money, housing, health or mental health, safety, relationships, plus "not sure" and "I'd rather talk to someone".

On today's tracing, the employment and prior support questions drop out (section 4.2). A mental health option is health data, so it needs the Data Minimisation Note and Appropriate Policy Document. Proposed: offer it with "prefer not to say", which broadens the results.

**What is shown.**
- Every place to start in the council, in the platform's own order: name, address, walk in or phone, and opening times exactly as listed.
- One fixed, reviewed sentence on what to expect.
- Housing Options and Shelter always shown alongside.

### 8.4 Open points for you

1. A safety pressure among several: does the Safety specialist lead, with the place to start second? (Proposed: yes.)
2. One pressure with no specialist in the council: a place to start, or the national floor?
3. No place to start in the council: show nothing of that kind and record the gap, or show a neighbouring council's, labelled?
4. Someone "nowhere near" crisis: ordinary search, or a place to start only if they name a difficulty?
5. Do places to start replace, sit beside or ignore the navigator list in prevention results?

The design note v2.1 you mentioned is not in either repo. If it is ready, it may already settle some of these. Two related housekeeping points:
- The working note on `feat/national-housing-2026-10-30` is the older July text. It lacks the consent rule, so it should be aligned before that branch merges.
- The Supporter and Professional Language Review v1.1 is a Word file in the project. Under the direction rule it belongs in the VA repo as Markdown.
